\begin{comment}
This ablation tests whether communication energy can be captured by a standard average-power accounting method, or whether explicit communication modeling is necessary. 
In tensor parallelism, AllReduce is not just a fixed energy addition. 
During ring AllReduce, GPUs exchange tensor chunks with neighboring GPUs across multiple steps. 
These transfers are not always perfectly aligned across ranks: if one GPU reaches the collective earlier or receives data later than another GPU, it may spend time idle while waiting for the communication step to complete. 
Thus, the energy of an AllReduce invocation depends not only on its elapsed time, but also on tensor size, communication volume, effective bandwidth, number of GPUs, and the ring communication pattern.
\end{comment}

Figure~\ref{fig:ablation_comm_modeling} shows that \textsc{AvgComm} incurs substantially higher prediction error of 34.0\% compared to \sys{}'s 12.9\%. 
The error increase is especially noticeable for larger models and 4-GPU settings, where AllReduce contributes a larger fraction of total energy. 
%%%This result shows that simply multiplying average communication power by communication time is not sufficient for accurate prediction.
The key distinction is that \sys{} models communication as a first-class module whose features reflect the actual communication. 
%%%By using tensor-derived byte volumes, calibrated effective bandwidth, and operation-specific communication parameters, \sys{} can distinguish between AllReduce invocations that have different transfer sizes and different communication behavior. 
The \textsc{AvgComm} ablation collapses these differences into a single average value, losing the structure needed to predict communication energy accurately.
